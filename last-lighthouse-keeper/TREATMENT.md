# The Last Lighthouse Keeper — treatment

Style: **Paper-cut Lightbox** (`paper-lantern`). Format: **YouTube Short, vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, ~41 s**, English narration (Kokoro, warm storyteller), burned-in subtitles + `.srt`, −14 LUFS.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The climb (vertical stack).** The tall frame is the tower. The camera starts at the sea, tilts up the stack as a lit stair-window climbs the tower, arrives at the lamp room, he lights the lamp, the beam reaches a ship, the ship answers. Uses the vertical format as the story's direction: climbing = the last duty.
2. **One window, one night (locked frame).** The lamp room seen from outside, locked; only light changes: dusk, his lantern arrives, the lamp lights, the beam turns, the ship's reply blinks in the glass reflection. Intimate, but static for a Short and the ship would be tiny.
3. **Two lights (cross-cut).** Alternate between two small boxes: the keeper's lamp room and a ship's bridge. He lights; the sailor sees it and flashes back. Clear, but splits a 40 s film in two and needs two figure rigs.

**Chosen: 1, with the ending of 3 folded in.** The vertical climb gives the Short its one continuous signature move (a tilt up a paper tower that fills the 9:16 frame), and the ship's reply arrives inside the same stack (a far layer on the horizon), so "two lights, one sea" happens without cutting away. Opening, ending and score shape come from the topic: dusk to night, a climb that accelerates, a held silence before the lamp, a swell on the light, a quiet walk home.

## 2. Logline and arc

On the last night before his lighthouse goes automatic, the old keeper climbs the tower and lights the lamp by hand one final time, and a passing ship flashes back two letters: *thank you*.

- **Setup**: dusk; a small lantern crosses the cliff path to the tower door. Tomorrow a machine takes over.
- **Turn**: the lamp room; a held breath of silence; his hand lights the lamp; the whole box comes alive.
- **Ending**: the beam crosses the sea, a ship blinks Morse "T U" back; the lantern goes down the path; the camera pulls out to the paper lamp itself, still glowing.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from**: Lotte Reiniger's silhouette films (readable jointed silhouettes, rotation-only motion); commercial paper-cut shadow-box lamps (value ladder, backlight bloom, shadow between sheets); Studio Ghibli-style quiet "work" sequences (the dignity of a repeated task, long holds); classic lighthouse documentary sound (sea bed, iron stairs, the lamp mechanism's clockwork).
- **Don't take**: any specific lamp design, film frame, character, melody or shot; nothing from the library demo (a train's last run).

## 8. Subtitles and titles

- Full-bleed shots: subtitles in a warm off-white serif (Cormorant Garamond 600), ~40 px at 1080 wide, soft shadow, on the dark front band ~150 px above the bottom (clear of YouTube Shorts' UI overlay at the very bottom), never over a lit window or the beam. A faint dark band where needed.
- Box framing (shot 6): under the box on the room's dark.
- Paper text: the end title THE LAST LIGHTHOUSE KEEPER is a cut paper strip grown from its left edge in the sky (not subtitled). The Morse strip is dots and dashes only.
- Numbers: spoken "forty-one", subtitled "41".
